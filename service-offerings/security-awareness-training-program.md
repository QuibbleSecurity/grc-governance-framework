## 1. Why this program exists

Most security breaches do not begin with a hacker defeating technology. They begin with a person — a convincing email, a fake wire instruction, a reused password. The large majority of breaches trace back to a human element, not a broken firewall.

Technology filters much of the noise, but the last line of defense is a trained, alert team that knows what a threat looks like and what to do about it. This program builds that layer, keeps it sharp with regular practice, and produces the paper trail that proves it is in place.

## 2. What's included

The program has three parts, delivered over a twelve-month term:

**Live, tailored training session** — A 45- to 60-minute session, onsite or virtual, built around the threats the business actually faces: phishing and social engineering, wire and payment fraud, handling client data, passwords and multi-factor authentication, device and physical security, and how to report something that looks wrong.

**Quarterly phishing simulations** — Realistic, controlled simulated phishing sent to the team each quarter. Anyone who clicks is automatically enrolled in a short refresher. There is no blame and no public list — it is coaching, and results improve fast once people know it is happening.

**Documentation package** — Sign-in sheets, knowledge-check results, completion certificates, and quarterly trend reports. Together these are the evidence file: proof of a running program for insurers, auditors, and client security questionnaires.

## 3. How the rollout works

The first month does the heavy lifting; after that the program settles into a light quarterly rhythm.

| Phase | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Kickoff & setup | Month 1, weeks 1–2 | Set up the client's private training workspace, load the team, and run a baseline phishing simulation *before* any training — so we know the starting point. |
| 2. Live session | Month 1, weeks 2–3 | Deliver the tailored session to the team, collect a sign-in sheet, run a short knowledge check, and issue completion certificates. |
| 3. Ongoing program | Every quarter thereafter | One phishing simulation per quarter, automatic refresher training for anyone who clicks, and a short report showing how the team is trending. Training content is refreshed annually. |

**Why the baseline comes first.** Running a simulation before any training gives an honest before-and-after. That first click rate is the number the quarterly reports measure against — and the clearest proof that the program is working.

## 4. What we need from the client

Getting started takes very little on the client's end. Quibble Security handles setup, delivery, tracking, and reporting.

- **Employee roster** — names, email addresses, and roles or departments.
- **Signed authorization** — written permission to run simulated phishing against staff. Quibble Security provides the form; it protects both parties.
- **A point of contact** — one person to coordinate scheduling for the live session.

## 5. What the client receives

Every part of the program leaves behind a tangible record. By the end of year one, the client holds:

- A baseline phishing simulation report
- The tailored live training session, delivered to the team
- A sign-in sheet and knowledge-check results for the session
- Completion certificates for every attendee
- Quarterly phishing simulation reports with trend data
- An annual program summary

**Why it matters.** Together these form a documented, defensible record of a working security-awareness program — exactly what a cyber-insurance carrier, an auditor, or an enterprise client wants to see when they ask how the business protects the data it holds.
